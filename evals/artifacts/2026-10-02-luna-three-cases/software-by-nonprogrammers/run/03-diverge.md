# Diverge — before broad research
Unpolished candidates, intentionally before source scanning:
1. Personal tool abundance: people translate local annoyances into one-off automations; maintenance becomes the hidden tax.
2. Domain workers become toolmakers: practical knowledge shapes workflows, reducing handoffs while elevating workers who can specify and inspect processes.
3. Abundance plus scarce trust: creation is cheap, but verification, identity, permissions, data stewardship and liability become scarce; the firm grows a control plane.
4. Software dissolves into adaptive interfaces: tools are generated in context and discarded, with less ownership of a “product.”
5. Counterpath: low-code islands stall or fracture under integrations, security, upkeep, and confusing responsibility; people retreat to vendors and specialists.
6. Identity/refusal: some workers value craft, stable procedures, or human accountability and reject generated tools; organizations preserve protected non-automated zones.
Mechanism variety: price/effort reduction; expertise and agency; governance/assurance; interface transformation; failure and lock-in; identity and refusal. These are hypotheses, not forecasts.
