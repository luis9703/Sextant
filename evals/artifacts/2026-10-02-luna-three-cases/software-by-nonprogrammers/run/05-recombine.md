# Recombine — after research
The research changes the candidates in three material ways. First, the evidence supports growing tool use among surveyed developers but says little about nonprogrammers; broad adoption remains a condition, not a starting fact. Second, friction and the METR counterexample make “creation gets cheap, therefore productivity rises” inadequate; testing, maintenance and context integration may consume savings. Third, NIST lifecycle practices and the NBER novice-support result suggest two new causal routes: verified building blocks can make small tools safe to share, and assistance can diffuse expert practices to domain workers. Revised candidates: (A) managed local-tool abundance through modular platforms and stewardship; (B) augmentation with human domain expertise and role shifts; (C) assurance bottleneck concentrates control in platforms/compliance or specialists; (D) failure-driven consolidation, with purposeful refusal as a live alternative. The dissolving-interface future survives only if generated experiences can reliably access data/actions, maintain state, and be accountable; otherwise it is a conditional branch, not baseline.
