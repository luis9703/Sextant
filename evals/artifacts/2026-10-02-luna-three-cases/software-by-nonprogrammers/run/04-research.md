# Research — cutoff 2026-10-02; web search and page text accessed 2026-10-02
Search was bounded to empirical productivity/adoption, trust, governance/security. Vendor and platform surveys have selection and self-report limitations; results below do not estimate nonprogrammer uptake.

- Stack Overflow, 2025 developer survey retrospective, https://stackoverflow.blog/2026/10/01/a-look-back-before-we-look-forward-a-developer-survey-retrospective/ (published Oct 1 2026; platform survey of 49k+ developers per survey framing). Reports 79% AI tool use in 2025, and trust/learning concerns; survey respondent base is developers, not general population. Supports diffusion within this sampled community alongside uncertainty, not generalized nonprogrammer adoption.
- Stack Overflow 2025 survey summary, https://stackoverflow.blog/2025/12/29/developers-remain-willing-but-reluctant-to-use-ai-the-2025-developer-survey-results-are-here/ (published Dec 29 2025): reports high tool use, but respondents noted “almost right” output and maintenance/debug burden; self-report and vendor/community audience caveat.
- GitHub enterprise team survey, https://github.blog/news-insights/research/survey-ai-wave-grows/ (2024, updated 2025; 2,000 enterprise software-team respondents in US, Brazil, Germany, India; ever-use question). Reports broad individual exposure but uneven organization support. Selection, self-report, and question wording limit generalization. Suggests individual capability may precede formal institutional adoption.
- Brynjolfsson, Li & Raymond, NBER WP 31161, https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w31161/revisions/w31161.rev1.pdf (field deployment involving 5,179 customer-support agents; revised Oct 2023). Access increased issues/hour by 14% average, with heterogeneous effects (larger novice/low-skilled, minimal for experienced/high-skilled). This is conversational support work, not software creation; demonstrates one mechanism for task assistance and skill diffusion, not universal productivity.
- METR randomized trial, https://metr.org/Early_2025_AI_Experienced_OS_Devs_Study-paper.pdf (16 experienced open-source developers, 246 mature-project tasks, early-2025 tools; paper reports 19% longer completion when allowed AI). Small, selected sample and codebase/task familiarity restrict scope; useful counterexample to “AI always speeds work.”
- NIST AI RMF GenAI Profile, https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf (2024 voluntary framework). Recommends testing, monitoring, provenance and operator proficiency; establishes that lifecycle assurance is recognized as work, not that any one governance arrangement will dominate.
- NIST SSDF GenAI profile, https://doi.org/10.6028/NIST.SP.800-218A (2024). Security practices remain applicable to AI-assisted software development; guidance is normative, not adoption measurement.

Closest existing accounts: “citizen development” and low-code/no-code already decentralize app creation; GitHub survey concerns developers/engineering teams, so does not demonstrate organizational citizen-developer outcomes. The novel question here is what changes if natural-language agents push creation further while ownership, support and assurance remain distributed.

Research challenge: observed trust/quality friction and a small RCT make universal acceleration doubtful. But these findings do not prove durable stagnation: task selection, tools, process, and user skill can change. Evidence on nonprogrammer-created tools’ longevity, incident rates, and firm structure is thin in this bounded search and remains unknown.
