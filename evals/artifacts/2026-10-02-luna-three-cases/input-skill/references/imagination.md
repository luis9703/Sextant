# Exploring beyond the first answer

Select operations that open different directions. Do not execute this as a fixed checklist. A candidate can begin as a few sentences: the assumption changed, the unfamiliar behavior or world, and the next thing worth finding out.

| Operation | Productive move | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assumption audit | Identify a shared assumption; explore when it stops holding or applies only to some people | Inventing a consensus, or reversing a well-supported fact just to be contrarian |
| Structural analogy | Transfer a relationship from a distant field, then state the mismatch | Comparing labels or appearances instead of mechanisms |
| Desire and identity | Explore who people could become, what they could express, and what they might reject | Treating all people as identical optimizers, or inventing cultural evidence |
| Capability and form | Remove a product name or interface; reorganize the function it serves | Renaming the same product while preserving its structure |
| Constraint and scarcity | Release a cost, delay, or limitation; identify the next bottleneck | Assuming every cost declines together or forever |
| Combination and institutions | Combine capabilities with ownership, trust, pricing, norms, or responsibility | Assuming that technical compatibility implies adoption |
| Scale and feedback | Move from one person's action to interacting people and organizations | Leaping to a societal effect without intermediate responses |
| Pre-mortem and alternatives | Imagine the path failed; use the missing factor to generate another path | A generic risk list, or claiming to enumerate all unknown unknowns |

For an analogy, retain three parts: source relationship, target relationship, failure boundary. An unfamiliar source field is not enough; its causal structure must matter. Verify any historical facts used as evidence during research.

During the second pass, compare the mechanisms after removing their names. Paths may overlap, but changes in who benefits or in adoption speed do not by themselves explore a different causal account. If the candidates all depend on the same explanation, investigate what could replace it or make it cease to dominate. Keep a brief account of the attempt and outcome; do not fill a quota or force an optimistic counterpart.

Ask what a newly found fact makes possible, whose experience was absent, and which initial assumption now looks unnecessary. A constraint can delay one path while opening another through changed behavior, design or institutions. Record when a research finding creates a new candidate rather than merely strengthening an old one.

A pathway may be useful without being unprecedented. Compare it with the closest existing discussion and explain the additional condition, mechanism, or implication. If removing a coined name removes its novelty, do not count it as a new mechanism. No discovery is better than manufactured novelty.

Keep speculative leaps visible. A path requiring a new scientific breakthrough belongs in a clearly conditional category. A path conflicting with established constraints should not be reported as plausible unless the relevant premise is explicitly changed into a hypothetical exercise.

Distinguish three kinds of constraint: the current product's form, institutions that could change, and well-established hard limits. The first two can motivate transitions into a different design or social arrangement. Do not reject an entire future class merely because today's product is small, expensive or designed for one use.

Deep work should develop the consequences of candidates rather than end after listing them. Follow the changed capability into behavior, relationships or institutions, and then into feedback and a new scarcity. Ask how people become different through use, how a refusal could spread, and how one actor's adaptation changes another's options. When a failure or released constraint opens a worthwhile alternative, follow it through these transitions too; saying that the original path disappears is not an account of the resulting future. Give important alternatives comparable development, while explaining differences in support. Show the conditions linking changes; do not simply add increasingly dramatic claims.

Use the creative challenge described in [review-and-updates.md](review-and-updates.md) before final delivery. Grounding and creative review should inform each other; neither a long source list nor absence of an existing example decides whether a conditional path is worth retaining.

These are proposed working techniques. Design fixation and iterative divergence/convergence provide motivation, not evidence that this particular LLM workflow improves creativity: [Jansson and Smith](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0142694X9190003F), [Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/).
