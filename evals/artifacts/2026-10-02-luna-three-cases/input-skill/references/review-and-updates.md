# Review without manufacturing certainty

A fresh reviewer receives the user question, scope and cutoff, neutral candidate summaries, assumptions, evidence and counterevidence, plus access to necessary sources. Exclude the generation history, author identity, rankings, and intended verdict. This removes one source of influence; it does not make a shared model or source statistically independent.

Use an isolated invocation or bounded subagent only when the host supports it and it is within the current authorization. Do not create a user-owned task, purchase tools, or contact others for this step. If unavailable, label the actual same-context review. Never claim an expert or independent review that did not occur.

Ask the reviewer to distinguish:

- A factual contradiction or a source that fails to support the premise.
- A missing transition, resource, actor response, cultural condition, or delay.
- A plausible alternative explanation or analogous case that fails differently.
- A speculative condition that can remain explicit without being called established.
- A repair that strengthens the candidate, or a failure that opens another path.

Record the challenge, its basis, the disposition, and remaining disagreement. Keep, revise, downgrade, drop, and unresolved are all valid. Use separate or mixed dispositions for separable issues. An unchanged probability can be justified. Newness is neither a reason to accept a claim nor a reason to reject it.

Save the actual reply and review packet. Reconciliation names the pathway and location of the concrete repair in the final draft, or explains why the objection remains; names keep the mapping intelligible when numbering changes. Inspect the final lead, affected transitions and observation plan, not just the disposition table. Check that withdrawn premises are no longer used, unresolved assumptions remain conditional, and accepted alternative branches were actually developed. Do not call a merely acknowledged objection resolved.

Reviewer recollections are research leads, not evidence. Spend remaining verification resources on leads that can materially change a path; preserve the checked source and label unresolved leads unverified. Check reviewer-proposed causal repairs too: does a signal distinguish competing explanations, does an example support the stated scope, and does a condition really exclude alternatives? A replacement mechanism is a candidate to examine, not authority to recast all futures around it. Never add all recalled sources just to make the report look researched. Preserve useful detail in phase artifacts while keeping the reader-facing report coherent. The user's scope and the Skill's evidence rules outrank a reviewer's request for false precision or extra work. If a new context completes reconciliation, record the handoff and exactly what it received.

Deep review covers two jobs: grounding and creative challenge. Grounding checks numbers, branch-specific dependencies, evidence status and whether observations distinguish alternatives. Creative challenge checks whether one causal account dominates all candidates, current-product constraints suppressed a broader future, human desires are assumed fixed, or a worthwhile failure branch remains only a footnote. It can propose another explanation or repair without demanding novelty or optimism. The same fresh reviewer may do both; the orchestrator launches reviewers directly rather than asking generating subagents to create nested reviewers.

If isolation or necessary verification is unavailable, record a partial review phase and its concrete limits. The receipt checker may verify other artifacts, but the run cannot claim full structural completion. It still may deliver useful conditional futures.

For public-facing claims, distinguish package validation, a worked example, model review, expert evaluation, and prospective prediction performance. None substitutes for the others. BTF-2 motivates attention to blind spots and strategic conditions but explicitly limits causal attribution of individual reasoning factors: [paper, discussion and limitations](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.26106v1).

When recording an actual binary forecast, define its question, scope, forecast timestamp, event deadline, probability basis, resolution source and rule, and treatment of ambiguous or unavailable outcomes. A draft may remain unquantified. Never interpret an unresolved event as false. Preserve old forecasts; changing the question requires an explicit new version. Scenario narratives need not become scoreable predictions.

For a later update, read the previous case before searching, state the newly available evidence, and identify what changed and what did not. Do not rewrite history to suggest a conclusion was held earlier. A signpost should specify how observing its opposite would change the assessment.

Project mechanism notes are optional and user-authorized. Each reusable lesson needs its conditions, source cases, counterexamples, discriminating signals, and status: hypothesis, supported observation, contested, or unknown. Repeated model outputs are not independent support, and one correct forecast does not establish its mechanism. Retrieve relevant notes after initial divergence to limit fixation; keep held-out evaluation answers and later outcomes out of the case's information set.

Scheduling, automatic learning, and public publication are separate capabilities. Do not imply that a list of observations has scheduled a monitor or that a Markdown lesson has trained the model.
