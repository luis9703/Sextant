# Research notes (accessed 2026-10-02)
1. European Commission, “Directive on repair of goods,” https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/consumer-protection-law/directive-repair-goods_en ; official policy page, accessed today. Reports Directive 2024/1799 applies from 31 Jul 2026; product categories depend on Annex II and product-specific EU reparability rules. EUR-Lex legal text https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/1799/oj/eng Articles 5–6 explicitly address 3D-printed spare parts when compliant with safety/IP law and repair obligations. Supports an institutional route for repair, not a universal right to print any item or proof of uptake.
2. Baumers et al? “Environmental Life Cycle Analysis of Distributed Three-Dimensional Printing and Conventional Manufacturing of Polymer Products,” ACS Sustainable Chem Eng (2013), https://pubs.acs.org/doi/full/10.1021/sc400093k . Search result/abstract read; 3 products, RepRap experimental process, ABS/PLA; benefit contingent on <25% infill PLA and energy assumptions. Full text open failed. Treat quantified result as abstract-reported, narrow and dated; no general environmental verdict.
3. Cerdas et al., “Life Cycle Assessment of 3D Printed Products in a Distributed Manufacturing System,” J Ind Ecol (2017), https://link.springer.com/article/10.1111/jiec.12618 . Article page accessed; compares specific system boundaries, regions and eight impact categories, supplementary ranges. Establishes that geography/system boundaries matter; not a blanket verdict.
4. CDC/NIOSH, “Safe 3D Printing is for Everyone, Everywhere” (2024), https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/bulletin/2024/safe-3d-printing.html and guide https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/docs/2024-103/ . Official occupational-health guidance: ultrafine particles/chemicals and hazards vary by printer-material combination; controls, ventilation, training matter. Home adoption adds air/safety cost, especially around children; this is not evidence of quantified household disease burden.
5. “Economic Impact of DIY Home Manufacturing…” (2022), https://ojs.unito.it/index.php/ejsice/article/view/6508 . Article page/abstract read: analyzes top 100 designs on YouMagine, a selection bias toward popular downloadable models; cannot represent typical household savings or total demand.
6. Fab City network, https://fab.city/network (initiative self-description, current page accessed today): 60+ territories and pledge; a civic intent/network signal, not measured local output. Near account: localized, globally connected production; novelty here is interaction with consumer repair/file custody.
7. Repair availability/latent barriers surfaced by 2026 Belgium “Hobby Repair” field-study article, https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S2667378926000167 ; page says three field trials, 2022–2026, labor costs, spare-part barriers, willingness-to-pay. Abstract/page only; location-specific and not evidence of broad replication. It suggests neighborhood repair service can be a stronger near-term pathway than each household learning CAD.
Counterevidence and limits: consumer designs demonstrate selected objects, not durable/critical parts; print emissions require controls; environmental effects may reverse with low machine utilization, failed prints, material production, electricity mix, postprocessing or freight displaced; repair still competes with replacement economics, warranty, skills and liability. No consistent household adoption or unit-cost time series established in this bounded search.
Search scope: official EU law, NIOSH, LCA and open-design/economic papers, Fab City program, one repair field trial. Not exhaustive, not cross-validated across independent experts. One ACS full text was inaccessible; relied only on returned abstract. Avoided projecting its percentage beyond its cases.
