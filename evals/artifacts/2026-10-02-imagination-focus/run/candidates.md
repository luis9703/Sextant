# First-pass candidates

Saved before broad research; each is conditional and may be wrong.

1. **A room becomes a message.** Capability migration: instead of measuring a room for a design job, capture its arrangement as context that another person can inspect asynchronously. A family member might show a visiting caregiver where everyday objects live, or ask a relative to help rearrange a cramped room. The new behavior is explaining by pointing in shared space rather than by a call and verbal directions. Key condition: lightweight capture plus simple annotations and private sharing.
2. **A home becomes a change log.** Repeated room captures let residents compare “before/after” for repairs, moves, renovations, or accessibility changes. This could shift contractors, landlords, insurers, and second-hand buyers toward documenting the state of a space. Key condition: useful comparability over time and accepted responsibility for records.
3. **Fit becomes a household service.** A portable room model lets retailers, delivery firms, movers, and repairers answer “will it fit / can we do the work here?” before a visit. The familiar logistics path is useful but comparatively obvious. Key condition: reliable dimensions, item models, and integrations.
4. **Shared spaces gain competing claims.** If scans make layouts easy to circulate, roommates, families, or building managers may negotiate furniture, storage, and access through visible proposals. The change could redistribute who gets to define a room’s “correct” use, not merely save measuring time. Key condition: multiple people can edit or contest the representation.

Initial development choice: candidate 1, because it transfers scanning from measurement into asynchronous human communication and makes a relational change imaginable. This is a focus choice, not an evidence-based ranking.
