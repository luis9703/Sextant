# Recombine after research

1. **Refined: nearby chosen-family web → care commons with human stewards.** The earlier candidate assumed that close friends simply begin reciprocal care. The Shanghai “older partners” study suggests a changed dependency: repeat contact, a coordinator and links to formal services make small networks able to notice needs and hand off tasks. The consequence is not a friendship app that replaces family; it is an everyday neighborhood layer in which friends, neighbors and workers coordinate while private homes remain separate. New risk: the steward becomes overburdened or an unpaid substitute for services.
2. **Retained, strengthened: shared infrastructure and separate doors.** Shanghai co-living research supports shared identity and pooled common goods while also documenting conflict over family expectations. The candidate now has two branches: voluntary younger-adult microhouseholds that treat shared meals and costs as practices of kinship, and purpose-built multigenerational/older-adult cohousing with explicit governance. Their added mechanism is repeated low-stakes shared use; care is still conditional on consent, governance, tenure security and an exit path.
3. **Revised: portable mediated support → “service floor plus chosen ties.”** Research on fragmented technology and volunteer fragility weakens the standalone-platform story. The changed dependency is reliable public/paid service handoffs plus consent-based tools, with friends contributing emotional and ordinary help. The result could be a portable support plan that follows people through moves without promising that distant ties can provide hands-on care. If services and interoperability fail, the network may be mostly alerts that do not lead to help.
4. **Develop as a real alternative: autonomy with professional support.** The original refusal candidate was a boundary condition. Research on people preferring independence and the cohabitation study's examples of rejecting formal commitment make it a distinct path: some remain solo by choice and select episodic professional care and low-obligation friendships. It succeeds only if affordability and access hold; otherwise apparent “choice” can become constrained isolation.
5. **Retain unresolved institutional branch: negotiated status or kinship lag.** Adult guardianship by written designation offers a limited instrument, but does not settle partner/friend status broadly. Over fifteen years, narrow tools may become more usable without reclassifying family law; alternatively institutions may still route emergencies and benefits through spouses/relatives, pushing some toward marriage, co-residence or private contracting. No evidence here settles policy direction.
