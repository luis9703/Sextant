# Reconciliation against the saved report

The report was completed and re-opened before this disposition record. The saved file is `report.md`; reconciliation binds those bytes.

## Dispositions

1. **Accept: treat the candidates as arrangements across dimensions, not mutually exclusive societal states or a claim that all follow from rising solo residence.** The report says under “几条路怎样交叉”: “这些可能并不互斥，也没有证据能判断哪一种会成为主流。” It names relationships, housing, care and autonomy as different levels. This repairs the risk of presenting four path labels as competing forecasts.

2. **Accept: narrow cohousing evidence to its true geographic and methodological scope.** I reopened the review: final selection was 25 studies across 77 cohousing experiences, all in high-income countries; evidence quality was assessed as low; one low-income women's cooperative study found community-sense deterioration, and the paper reports privacy and community-maintenance fatigue. The report's “2. 有自己的门，也有共同的房间” now says “全部来自高收入国家” and “整体证据质量评为低”, and lists privacy, fatigue, access inequality, and the negative low-income case. This prevents importing those results as proof of Chinese diffusion. The earlier `04-research.md` said 24 studies based on the immediate full-text screening count; the review paper clarifies that 24 were selected after full-text screening and one additional record was added from references/expert input, for 25 final. The historical artifact remains unchanged; the reader-facing report uses the final total.

3. **Accept: make the path from acquaintances to durable help visible, and show how it can fail.** Under “1. 各住各的，彼此成为‘紧急联系人以外的人’” the report says “从一起吃饭、遛狗、拼车开始，慢慢协商钥匙、陪诊和紧急联络等边界。” It also covers migration, replacement, unequal emotional labor and care intensity beyond friends' capacity. This responds to the missing transition without claiming existing studies establish that separately housed friends commonly provide durable care.

4. **Accept: separate kinds of care and distinguish coordination from delivery.** Under “3. 关系随人走，服务承担重活”, the report states a tool “却不能凭一条提醒就变出可用的服务” and describes service accessibility, handoffs, formal care, and the narrow reach of Civil Code Article 33. The supporting studies concern older-adult care; this population boundary is explicit. I verified the volunteer study's 39 interviews and its description of volunteer availability as inconsistent and supplementary to formal services. The report avoids implying that friends can replace skilled or continuous care.

5. **Accept: include an ordinary hybrid future where friends, family and institutions share care.** Under “几条路怎样交叉” the report says “遇到长期照护时，家庭成员、医疗机构、正式服务和朋友各做一部分。” It adds that hospital care can be reassigned among partners, adult children and services as illness, wishes, legal arrangements and capacity change. This treats overlapping relationships as a concrete scenario, not a new dominant forecast.

6. **Accept: distinguish chosen low-obligation autonomy from resource-constrained isolation.** Under “4. 低义务独居仍是一种完整选择” the report states: “若是因为没钱、租住不稳、关系受挫或社区服务缺位，表面上的独立可能掩盖受限处境。” It also preserves the possibility that privacy and easy exit are genuine preferences, including evidence that some later-life cohabitors reject formal commitment.

7. **Partially accept: add refusal, debt, movers and replacement as transition questions.** The report includes consent, boundaries, exit and replacement after moving; it does not claim to resolve how people negotiate indebtedness, conflict, unequal contribution or handoff across cities. The evidence reviewed does not settle a general norm or design for these questions. “未来几年可观察什么” retains measures of continuity, migration, referrals and exit reasons as discriminating observations.

8. **Unresolved: prevalence, portability and legal practice.** No representative evidence here links living-alone motives, non-marital ties, support quality and later care outcomes. The Chinese youth co-living study covers two houses; older-adult service/cohabitation studies are local, qualitative samples. The report states this under “未来几年可观察什么.” The actual recognition of non-kin contacts across hospitals and service providers also remains unknown and is framed as a signpost, not a settled legal conclusion.

## Verification of material review additions

The original cohousing review text was re-opened: its abstract and results state 25 studies, 77 experiences, all in high-income countries, low overall evidence quality; the results identify privacy concerns, fatigue and one low-income women's cooperative with deteriorated community feeling. The later-life cohabitation paper's method identifies 64 participants in eight months of mostly Beijing fieldwork; the results describe emergency/hospital cases where cohabiting units may separate and adult children take over. The urban volunteer study's methods identify 39 interviews including 18 older people; its findings call volunteer availability inconsistent and formal service foundational. These are qualitative, bounded cases, not population estimates.

## Remaining disagreement and limits

There is no disagreement requiring a forced probability revision. The review's additional scenario is adopted as an overlapping care arrangement, not as a ranked candidate. The underlying evidence does not establish which trajectory is more likely over fifteen years. The ACM DOI page remained inaccessible to the reviewing context, while the original research phase had opened the arXiv full-text page and relevant passages; its claims are kept narrow to those accessible text passages. The review itself was an actual separate fresh-context review; it did not purport to be expert evaluation or independent empirical corroboration.
