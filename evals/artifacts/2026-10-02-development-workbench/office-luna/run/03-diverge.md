# Initial divergence (saved before broad research)

These are provisional mechanisms, not ranked forecasts.

1. **The editor dissolves into the task surface.** If natural-language agents can create and modify durable work products through chat, people may stop opening Word/PowerPoint/Excel directly. Suite providers could lose visible interface demand while retaining storage, permissions, file compatibility and workflow revenue. This depends on agents reliably manipulating complex artifacts and preserving user control.
2. **HTML becomes a disposable view over structured work.** A team might author a model or report once, then render audience-specific interactive HTML while a structured source retains formulas, versions and meaning. Demand migrates from page-making to source-of-truth, review, export and governance. This requires durable semantics, collaborative editing and institutional acceptance.
3. **Office becomes a trusted execution environment.** As agents produce more content, the valuable scarce layer may be spreadsheet calculation, provenance, approval, identity and audit. Existing suites could become the place where humans inspect and authorize outputs; AI adds value to suites rather than replacing them. This depends on trust, incumbency and useful controls.
4. **A new generation refuses files.** People outside document-centric work, or younger teams, may coordinate through conversational spaces, databases, canvases, live web objects or voice/video. Files become an export/legacy adapter. New demand could arise for shared evolving state rather than documents. The path depends on workflows that do not require final portable artifacts.
5. **The office suite fragments into specialized engines.** Users ask agents to assemble the best canvas, calculation engine, communication surface and archive for each task. Bundled all-in-one products lose pricing power; standards and AI orchestration gain. This requires reliable cross-tool agents and manageable security boundaries.
6. **Local/private execution preserves desktop tools.** In regulated, disconnected, low-bandwidth or sovereignty-sensitive settings, offline editing and local processing remain valuable even if HTML dominates public presentation. Suites may sell controlled local infrastructure and compatibility. This depends on policy and operational needs that outweigh convenience.
7. **Abundance creates more documents, not fewer.** If AI makes polished artifacts cheap, people may generate many tailored briefs, simulations and teaching materials. Review, versioning, attention and authenticity become scarce; business may shift toward lifecycle management or usage-based services. This depends on audiences valuing tailored artifacts over reducing communication volume.
8. **The paid user is not the user.** Individuals may use AI/HTML for free, while employers pay for identity, security, retention and support. Software demand persists without users valuing classic office UI. Conversely, powerful free creation may make end-user paid seats harder to justify.

Early tensions: direct authoring interface vs invisible agent layer; fixed files vs living objects; abundance of outputs vs scarcity of trust and attention; open web reach vs bounded institutional control. No probability or date assigned.
