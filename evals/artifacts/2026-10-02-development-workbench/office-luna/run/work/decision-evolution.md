# Evolution follow-up: approval changes a real-world allocation

Temporary assumptions: the manager sees a trustworthy recommendation with source lineage; delegated execution has explicit limits; each approval stores the evidence, alternatives and permissions visible at that moment; the institution accepts that receipt as a valid record. This pass explores an event, not an observed deployment.

## An ordinary episode and the next cycle

A regional manager approves shifting inventory from one area to another based on a demand estimate. The workflow system records the selected quantity, excluded alternatives, data timestamp, agent explanation and the manager's approval. A downstream operations service executes within the approved cap. The manager does not ask an analyst to make a monthly slide first; she still wants a concise account of why the system proposed the move and how to stop it.

Next cycle, performance is below the expected range. Frontline staff point out that a local promotion was missing from the source. The reviewer replays the prior receipt and confirms the promotion was absent at approval time. This resolves what the manager saw, but not whether the decision was reasonable, whether the data owner failed to report the promotion, or whether the model was overconfident. The system flags other decisions using the affected feed, halts those still reversible, and sends a correction request to the data owner. Affected managers receive a short impact note; they choose which actions to reverse. One supervisor asks for a free-text dissent field because the missing context was known orally but not represented in the data.

Repeated episodes alter routine: a decision receipt becomes a compact attachment to an operational event; a monthly deck shifts from recounting all activity to explaining unusual outcomes, unresolved objections and decisions that need leadership attention. The analyst's role may move from copying metrics to maintaining definitions and challenging explanations. The frontline worker gains a way to make context visible, but faces pressure to formalize every exception. Managers gain speed yet inherit visible responsibility for choosing among system alternatives.

## Two branches from the same miss

- **Learning branch:** correction requests become routine, the organization adds a temporary event field and tracks whether unresolved objections predict poor decisions. The next cycle routes only decisions with missing context to human review. Repetitive status decks shrink, while exception explanations and traceable data ownership grow. New scarcity is adjudicating which context is decision-relevant and who is accountable for a data gap.
- **Defensive branch:** the receipt records approval but managers fear that an incomplete record will later be treated as proof of negligence. They ask analysts to write lengthy rationale before each approval and preserve screenshots, emails and spreadsheets outside the workflow. The monthly report grows as a shield even though execution is automated. New demand is for defensive narratives and external legal/audit interpretation; the workflow cannot replace file work.
- **Refusal branch:** frontline staff avoid entering local knowledge because it creates accountability without giving them power to change the system. They keep side spreadsheets or tell managers privately. The formal receipt remains precise but systematically incomplete. Managers return to a document meeting because it allows negotiation and lets uncertainty be voiced. This is a social failure despite technically reliable data.

## Bridge, alternative and reversal

A plausible entry begins with bounded, reversible decisions already made from structured operational data. Organizations first generate receipts beside existing approval processes; then use them to investigate corrections; then allow low-risk actions to execute under caps. Only when actors accept the receipt and the appeal path do some monthly reports lose their coordinating role. This is not simply an Office feature becoming better.

The path reverses if the appeal route is weak, unstructured knowledge is systematically excluded, wrong actions cannot be undone, responsibility follows the approver without control over inputs, or cross-functional negotiation is itself the purpose of the report. Conversely, an institution may keep the workflow system and still retain Office/WPS for planning, disagreement and communication.

Discriminating observations in a real workflow would include: whether the canonical record is the event receipt or an attached file; how many decisions were replayed, corrected, reversed or appealed; whether the receipt exposes data omissions; what extra explanation staff create after exceptions; and whether dissent fields are used by actors who have power to affect outcomes. A drop in presentation count alone cannot distinguish learning from suppressed voice.
