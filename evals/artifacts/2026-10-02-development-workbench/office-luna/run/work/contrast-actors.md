# Contrast pass: same monthly decision, different constraints

Situation held constant: a monthly performance result must inform a decision and be shared with relevant people. Choices are a live typed source with audience views, a static HTML report, an Office-compatible workbook/deck, or no formal report. “Who uses” and “who pays” are distinct.

| Setting and actors | Conditional choice and trade-off | Demand and value capture | What reverses it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Internal team with shared data systems.** Analyst prepares; managers decide; IT/security sets policy; employer pays. | If the data definitions are stable and all readers have managed access, a live object is attractive because corrections propagate and readers can inspect definitions. Office remains useful for exceptions, formulas/layout and offline work. No report is feasible when the decision is already visible in a workflow dashboard. | Need: decision and accountability. Use: analysts/managers consume, edit or inspect. Payment: employer/department buys suite, cloud, controls or an agent. Capture may accrue to incumbent suite or data-workflow platform; analyst need not be buyer. | External parties, unreliable lineage, sensitive permissions, or an audit rule requiring a frozen snapshot make an Office/PDF export with an approval record preferable. |
| **Regulated organization or external counterparty.** A reviewer may bear legal/accountability risk while another party creates the report; IT procurement approves vendor. | If evidence must remain inspectable after policy, personnel and software changes, a signed/versioned file plus evidence bundle can dominate a mutable link. If both parties share a governed tenant and can preserve versioned views, live collaboration can reduce reconciliation effort. | Need: defensible proof, retention and exchange. Use: analysts and reviewers. Payment: institution, sometimes procurement bundles. Value capture depends on retained access, controls, interoperability and support more than authoring keystrokes. | Common standards, trusted cross-tenant provenance and accepted long-term preservation can shift toward live objects; policy restrictions or vendor lockout reverse toward exportable files. |
| **Small independent operator or creator.** One person creates; clients consume; the creator decides whether to pay. | If a polished interactive update link brings audience engagement and there are few internal controls, HTML or a platform page is cheaper to distribute. If a client requests a spreadsheet/deck, or the operator needs advanced calculations, an Office app or compatible file returns. No formal artifact is rational for a low-stakes one-off. | Need/use can be high while willingness to pay is low. The operator may pay for an AI service or a free/low-cost suite; a platform may capture value through distribution/ads/transactions rather than office subscription. | Repeated client edits, portable archive needs, advanced native features or loss of platform access make suite subscription worthwhile; a free agent that exports well can reduce payment. |

The observed product sources show current offerings cover web, desktop, offline, file editing, collaboration and governance, but they do not reveal the comparative willingness to pay for each setting. Choice turns on who carries the cost of an error or an inaccessible artifact. A static HTML report is not automatically an editable spreadsheet; a mutable live page is not automatically a valid retained record. No universal provider winner follows.
