# Evolution pass: live decision object replacing the monthly deck

Temporary conditions for this exploration: a finance team can bind data fields and calculation logic to a typed source; an agent can create audience-specific web views with working permissions, citations and change history; the recipient organization accepts a signed/auditable event record as evidence of what was reviewed. These are assumptions, not established current capabilities at this quality level.

## Ordinary episodes and feedback

An analyst refreshes a monthly performance view. Rather than copy numbers into a slide deck, she checks an agent-prepared explanation, flags one source mismatch, and publishes a link. A regional manager opens a view that emphasizes their territory, changes a filter, and leaves a question anchored to a metric. The analyst corrects the lineage once; every audience view inherits the correction. Because a link is cheaper than a bespoke deck, the manager forwards it to a partner, who requests a restricted view instead of another file.

On the next cycle, the team compares the current result with last month's exact definition and sees which assumptions changed. Recipients begin preparing questions against the shared object before the meeting. The meeting shifts from walking slides to deciding which source or threshold to change. The analyst's prestige may shift from slide polish toward being trusted to define measures and explain exceptions. This is a specific organizational hypothesis, not a population-wide claim.

Repeated use creates a new scarcity: deciding who may derive a view, whose definition is authoritative, and whether an agent-edited explanation reflects the calculation or only narrates it. An access mistake can expose sensitive segments; an unnoticed source update can rewrite the apparent past. Managers may therefore ask for immutable snapshots at decision time. The live object acquires a versioned “what was known/approved then” receipt. Demand has not vanished; it moved from composing the deck toward source stewardship, permissioning and audit.

## Failure and alternative

If a recipient lacks reliable connectivity, trusts only signed attachments, cannot inspect the calculation, or has a legal retention rule tied to fixed artifacts, the link is exported to a spreadsheet/PDF/deck with provenance and approved at a human checkpoint. That exception becomes a recurring parallel route, potentially preserving Office-compatible editing and export. A failure of live sharing can also make the team prefer a local replica or generated static report rather than abandon the underlying typed source.

## Bridge back to present

A plausible entry is an internal recurring report with already structured data and known stakeholders. First, current report templates become data-backed views; next, teams adopt semantic definitions and version histories; then recipient-specific views are granted by role; only after audit acceptance do some meetings stop treating a slide deck as the canonical record. Existing HTML dashboards and office exports offer an incremental path, while file formats and app compatibility can remain adapters.

Reversal conditions: an agent's explanations drift from the source calculation; permission errors outweigh customization value; the organization cannot preserve past versions; recipients cannot comment or export reliably; or external counterparties still require stable office files. This is an alternate workflow, not evidence of broad adoption or guaranteed suite disappearance.
