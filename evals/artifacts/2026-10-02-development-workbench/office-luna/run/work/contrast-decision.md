# Contrast follow-up: same allocation decision, three ways of working

Situation held constant: a manager must allocate a scarce resource using the same trustworthy evidence, then explain the choice to affected teams. The three options are suite plus agent, workflow approval/execution with on-demand export, or a human-authored document meeting.

| Option | Conditional choice and who does what | Use, payment and capture | Reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| Suite plus agent | Prefer when the manager and analyst need to inspect alternatives in a workbook or negotiate a presentation narrative before committing. The analyst operates the agent and repairs its work; manager approves; affected teams consume the artifact. | Employer pays for suite and perhaps agent license; analyst and manager use it. Suite provider can capture subscription/AI fees, but the analyst bears review effort and the operational team bears allocation consequences. | If every decision is routine, reversible and data-backed, the meeting and workbook become overhead; execution inside workflow with a short receipt becomes attractive. |
| Workflow decision with on-demand export | Prefer when inputs, limits, owner and execution are already machine-readable; authorization can be replayed; a missing-data path and appeal route exist. Manager approves, service executes within limits, and affected teams receive an explanation plus a receipt. | Employer or business unit pays for workflow/data/identity controls, not necessarily an office editor. Operator may be an administrator or analyst; frontline users can consume free. Value can accrue to the workflow platform or cloud suite if it owns the event, identity and storage. | A need to compare editable scenarios across parties, narrate conflicting goals, or record dissent outside predefined fields flips the choice toward a workbook/document meeting. |
| Human-authored document meeting | Prefer when the decision depends on competing interpretations and the group needs to negotiate which evidence matters. The analyst authors; manager convenes/approves; affected teams question and revise the rationale. | Employer pays staff time and possibly suite subscriptions. Direct use may be concentrated in the analyst while the cost of delay falls on operating teams. Provider value capture may remain bundled and hard to attribute. | If evidence and policy become stable, participants accept explicit dissent fields and decisions can be reversed, workflow approval reduces repeated manual reconstruction. |

The most important switch is whether the group must negotiate unstructured disagreement before it can choose. Holding the evidence accurate and permissions stable, requiring visible dissent and narrative persuasion favors a human document/meeting; accepting structured dissent, capped delegation and an appeal path favors workflow execution. This does not show that current organizations have made that switch.

Need can remain while office-product payment weakens: workflow users still need explanations, but a platform can bundle export; employers may keep only a few advanced author seats; recipients may never pay. Conversely, recipients who must independently edit calculations can justify Office/WPS-compatible tools even when they did not create the original file. Evidence needed includes the actual procuring unit, license assignment by role, operating time, and where export/editing is performed. Vendor segment revenue alone cannot distinguish the alternatives.
