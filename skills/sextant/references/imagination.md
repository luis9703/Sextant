# Exploring beyond the first answer

Select operations that open different directions. Do not execute this as a fixed checklist. A candidate can begin as a few sentences: what ability or arrangement escapes its present use, what unfamiliar behavior or world becomes conceivable, and what connecting condition matters most. Early hypotheses may be wrong; make them worth developing before narrowing the set.

| Operation | Productive move | Common failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Assumption audit | Identify a shared assumption; explore when it stops holding or applies only to some people | Inventing a consensus, or reversing a well-supported fact just to be contrarian |
| Structural analogy | Transfer a relationship from a distant field, then state the mismatch | Comparing labels or appearances instead of mechanisms |
| Desire and identity | Explore who people could become, what they could express, and what they might reject | Treating all people as identical optimizers, or inventing cultural evidence |
| Capability migration | Remove the product and industry label; identify the operation it makes cheap or possible and find a distant use with that dependency | Forecasting more of today's use, or renaming the same product |
| Constraint and scarcity | Release a cost, delay or limitation; explore newly worthwhile activity and the next scarce input | Assuming all costs decline together, or always ending at approval and verification |
| Complementary breakthroughs | Combine a capability with changes in methods, data, materials, interfaces, distribution or institutions; identify the threshold their conjunction crosses | Treating one breakthrough as sufficient, or listing technologies without the interaction |
| Created demand | Ask what people would begin doing, wanting or identifying with once an activity becomes accessible, repeatable or abundant | Assuming future demand is today's demand multiplied |
| Scale and feedback | Move from one person's action to interacting people and organizations | Leaping to a societal effect without intermediate responses |
| Pre-mortem and alternatives | Imagine the path failed; use the missing factor to generate another path | A generic risk list, or claiming to enumerate all unknown unknowns |
| Backward bridge | Start from a striking conditional future and work back to the few changes that could connect it to the seed | Treating an appealing endpoint as proof or adding unlimited assumptions to rescue it |

A useful creative spine is capability → unfamiliar use → complementary changes → new behavior or demand → feedback, scarcity and wider reorganization. This is a reasoning aid, not a mandatory output schema. For a social seed, start from a changed relationship, norm or form of coordination rather than forcing a technological explanation. A credible bridge can contain assumptions about things not yet observed; make those assumptions visible instead of demanding a citation for the future itself.

For an analogy, retain three parts: source relationship, target relationship, failure boundary. An unfamiliar source field is not enough; its causal structure must matter. Verify any historical facts used as evidence during research.

During the second pass, compare the mechanisms after removing their names. Paths may overlap, but changes in who benefits or in adoption speed do not by themselves explore a different causal account. If the candidates all depend on the same explanation, investigate what could replace it or make it cease to dominate. Keep a brief account of the attempt and outcome; do not fill a quota or force an optimistic counterpart.

The candidate ledger carries this comparison across steps. Quote each core from its saved source rather than paraphrasing it, so later steps can see what the idea actually was. A merge is complete when the receiving pathway contains the core as a concrete sentence, scene or branch; a core listed only as background or as context for another pathway is set aside, with its reason. Set-aside rows go to review as material the creative challenge can revive. A short table is enough: id, quoted core, state, and the receiving pathway or the reason.

Ask what a newly found fact makes possible, whose experience was absent, and which initial assumption now looks unnecessary. A constraint can delay one path while opening another through changed behavior, design or institutions. For an added or replaced candidate, compare the earlier mechanism with the changed dependency or actor response and the new consequence. An extra coordinator or institution can matter, but explain how it changes choices, power, costs or failure handling. If the mechanism is substantially the same, describe the work as refinement rather than claiming another causal route.

A pathway may be useful without being unprecedented. Compare it with the closest existing discussion and explain the additional condition, mechanism, or implication. If removing a coined name removes its novelty, do not count it as a new mechanism. No discovery is better than manufactured novelty.

Keep speculative leaps visible. A path requiring a new scientific breakthrough belongs in a clearly conditional category. A path conflicting with established constraints should not be reported as plausible unless the relevant premise is explicitly changed into a hypothetical exercise.

Distinguish three kinds of constraint: the current product's form, institutions that could change, and well-established hard limits. The first two can motivate transitions into a different design or social arrangement. Do not reject an entire future class merely because today's product is small, expensive or designed for one use.

Turn a consequential current property into a variable in the exploration, rather than silently freezing it for the whole horizon. Choose what matters for this candidate: ownership, scale, location, interfaces, visibility, who may use it, or another relevant arrangement. Hold the other conditions intelligible and develop what becomes possible, unnecessary or newly desired when that property changes. The resulting design may still need engineering or institutional changes; keep those as conditions. This is a selective operation, not a parameter table to fill or a claim that all limitations disappear together.

When a current premise or reviewer-proposed constraint fails, separate it from the future it was meant to support. Try a warranted alternative bridge if the future is still worth exploring; the result may be a transformed path rather than a polished version of the same story. A feature count, present workflow or convenient vignette is not proof that two uses exclude each other. If the mechanism truly requires a contradicted premise, preserve the failed attempt instead of manufacturing a rescue.

For a broad seed with meaningful alternative directions, use authorized fresh contexts with the [selected-input method](independent-exploration.md). Compare mechanisms after results return. Do not pre-assign a winner or feed every explorer the first preferred account. A same-context additional pass remains useful but is not unexposed independent generation.

Deep work should develop the consequences of candidates rather than end after listing them. Follow the changed capability into behavior, relationships or institutions, and then into feedback and a new scarcity. Ask how people become different through use, how a refusal could spread, and how one actor's adaptation changes another's options. When a failure or released constraint opens a worthwhile alternative, follow it through these transitions too; saying that the original path disappears is not an account of the resulting future. Give important alternatives comparable development, while explaining differences in support. Show the conditions linking changes; do not simply add increasingly dramatic claims.

For a promising distant branch, use the [development workbench](development.md) to follow events after explicit enabling assumptions hold, then bridge back. This is the same creative operation, performed once; do not repeat a vignette merely to produce a second required file. Earlier genuine work can be adopted transparently. Let one result change the next worthwhile question; a list with more qualifiers is not necessarily deeper exploration.

Use the creative challenge described in [review-and-updates.md](review-and-updates.md) before final delivery. Grounding and creative review should inform each other; neither a long source list nor absence of an existing example decides whether a conditional path is worth retaining.

These are proposed working techniques. Design fixation and iterative divergence/convergence provide motivation, not evidence that this particular LLM workflow improves creativity: [Jansson and Smith](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/0142694X9190003F), [Design Council](https://www.designcouncil.org.uk/resources/framework-for-innovation/).
